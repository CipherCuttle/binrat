## Problem and bounded change

The current live canary audits every 60 seconds in `src/portable/liveSourceV2.ts`. The retained model requires at least 15,840 requests/day (11 × 1,440), before other usage. Raising the timer alone cannot establish viability: the authority validator caps audits at 60 seconds, the cost grant caps lifetime at 180 seconds, and covering the sampled 859,315 blocks/day with 50,000-block ranges still needs 18 ranges/day and repeated verification reads. See [the current budget blocker](TRIPWIRE_LIVE_SOURCE_V2_CANARY.json) and [the wide-range model](ORBBOOK_WIDE_RANGE_PROBE.md).

The smallest coherent proposal is **one separately versioned positive-event Watch contract and its budgeted scheduler**. Keep one exact-deployer WebSocket subscription armed, remove periodic HTTP range audits from this new mode, and verify only notified candidate blocks. Quiet and disconnected intervals remain `UNKNOWN_COVERAGE`; connection health never means complete coverage or absence of launches. This explicit contract requires owner approval before implementation and cannot replace the current coverage contract silently.

Proposed files are new, non-frozen modules: `src/portable/persistentTripwireWatch.ts` (policy, scheduler and candidate proof), `src/portable/persistentTripwireBudget.ts` (durable cost/slot reservations against the existing reconciled ledger), and `test/portablePersistentTripwireWatch.test.ts`. Reuse existing immutable decoding, receipt-root and identity helpers. Preserve frozen V3/V4/V5 bytes. Keep the implementation inert and unmounted; owner HTTP/UI composition, Telegram delivery and production activation remain separate gates.

The scheduler would:

- Permit one owner, deployer, existing provider and a finite, separately granted 24-hour lifetime. Do not extend the existing 180-second canary grant.
- Reserve at most eight HTTP reads for fresh admission and source pins, plus one connection attempt, subscribe and terminal unsubscribe: **11 shared slots**. Failure to fit the actual admission sequence blocks admission.
- Coalesce duplicate hints by canonical block. A candidate proof uses at most six physical requests: fresh latest and finalized headers; candidate header; exact-block filtered logs; complete ordered block receipts; candidate header recheck. Require the existing finality/two-block rules, receipt root, exact filtered/receipt-log equality, consent boundary and original identities before accepting a positive result. If the block is not finalized, retain it as unverified; wait for a later independently admitted candidate job or explicit poll rather than starting a retry timer. Verification latency is therefore unbounded during quiet periods.
- Bound one proof job to 60 seconds and one attempt/request. Persist pending hints, terminal outcomes and every debit across restart. Reconnects consume existing remaining allowances; never acquire a fresh daily allowance by restarting or changing grant identity.
- Keep total shared usage, including prior diagnostics, at ≤100/UTC day. Admit a candidate job only when its full six-slot/CU allowance and terminal unsubscribe are available. Exhaustion disables further candidate work and closes the subscription; do not promise unlimited event volume.
- Independently retain the 10,000 modeled CU / $0.01 estimated ceiling for the entire proposed grant, with durable delivery-byte accounting. Reserve the existing 64 KiB + 8 KiB frame allowance before connection (2,949.12 modeled CU); retain the existing 8 KiB/frame stop rule. Charge unavoidable delivered overflow and stop. Provider invoice and protocol/TLS overhead remain unverified; modeled bounds are not invoice guarantees.

## Measurable offline acceptance

1. A simulated quiet 24-hour grant performs no periodic audit or catch-up HTTP requests after admission. Its planned setup/teardown fits ≤11 shared slots. Every quiet/disconnected interval is explicitly unknown.
2. Each accepted distinct candidate block costs ≤6 physical requests and ≤400 modeled request CU at the retained tariff (four header reads ×50 CU + logs 100 CU + receipts 100 CU). No raw hint creates a verified positive or notification eligibility.
3. For `U` prior shared debits and `P` candidate jobs, the planned request bound is **U + 11 + 6P ≤100**. On an otherwise unused day, an example cap of 12 candidate jobs uses 83 slots. Today, with U=13, that example uses 96. These are admission limits, not guarantees of finalized events or future capacity.
4. The conservative corresponding model is **2,949.12 + 860 + 400P CU**: stream envelope, up to eight 100-CU admission reads plus three 20-CU connection/subscription operations, and candidate jobs. Twelve jobs reserve at most **8,609.12 CU / $0.00860912**. Every operation must also fit the independently enforced actual grant; other usage and reconnects reduce capacity.
5. A 99/100 ledger, exhausted CU/byte allowance, duplicate hint storm, reconnect, crash/restart, UTC boundary and concurrent reservation race cause no unreserved IO or double allowance. Persist all interrupted attempts; retain terminal teardown capacity. Grant expiration/cancellation fences every pending callback and closes resources.
6. Fixture controls reject omitted or reordered receipts, root mismatch, filtered-log omission, changed headers, pre-consent events, removed logs, unavailable finality and unsupported methods. Pending finality never starts repeated polling. Restart cannot turn historical or missed intervals into verified coverage.
7. All tests use fake time and in-memory transports with network denied. No production route is mounted, no Watch is created, and no Telegram/outbox delivery occurs. A green offline result does not qualify the live source or authorize activation.

If continuous exhaustive coverage with bounded detection latency must remain the contract, this positive-event proposal does not satisfy it. A timer-only adjustment has no demonstrated solution within 100/day. V5 source qualification and the Pons clean-stop condition remain unresolved before another live maintenance execution.
